Use case name: EditArticleTitleAndTags
Description: The author can change the title and the tags of an existing article. A new title gives the article a new address derived from that title, and the tags shown everywhere follow the edit.
Participating Actors:
- Author: a signed-in user who wrote the article.
Entry conditions: The Author is signed in and the article has at least one tag, at least one comment and at least one favorite.
Flow of events User System:
1. The Author opens one of their own articles and chooses to edit it.
2. The system shows the editor filled with the current title, description, body and tags.
3. The Author changes the title, removes one tag, adds a new tag and publishes the change.
4. The system saves the change, gives the article a new address derived from the new title, and shows the updated article at that new address with the new tags.
5. The Author returns to the home page.
6. The system lists the article with its new title and new tags; the popular tags include the new tag.
Alternative flows:
A1. The Author changes only the body. The system keeps the title, the address and the tags unchanged.
A2. The Author removes every tag. The system saves the article without tags.
A3. Somebody opens the article's previous address. The system answers that the article was not found.
A4. The Author gives the article a title that another article already uses. The system refuses the change, tells the Author that the title is already taken, and the article stays unchanged.
A5. An edit request sent directly to the API without a tag list keeps the existing tags; one with an empty tag list removes all tags; one whose tag list is not a list of words is refused as invalid (HTTP 422) and changes nothing.
Exit conditions:
- The article keeps its comments, its favorites and its author after the title and address change.
- The tags of the article are exactly the ones the Author left in the editor.
Quality requirements:
- After a successful save the browser address bar shows the new article address, so reloading the page shows the same article.
